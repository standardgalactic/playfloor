\chapter{The Five Failure Modes}
\index{failure modes}

\section{Failure as Information}

A reconstruction failure is not the absence of an explanation. It is itself an explanation. The framework classifies failures precisely---distinguishing architectural mistakes from structural limits of the trajectory space.

The key distinction: \textbf{architectural failures} arise from design choices that can be improved. \textbf{Structural failures} arise from properties of the trajectory space that cannot be engineered away.

\section{Stability Failure}
\index{failure modes!stability}

Stability failure occurs when the stability sequence $\{\sigma_r\}$ is not summable. The compressibility error $E_c = C_Q \sum_{r>d} \sigma_r$ does not converge to zero at any finite truncation order. This is a property of the trajectory space for the query family---no compression, however well-designed, can remedy it.

\textbf{Examples.} Learning: derivative tensors fail to decay. Semantic: $B\alpha \geq 1$. Physical: Outcome C. Memory: trauma-related queries may exhibit this pattern.

\textbf{Classification: Structural.}

\section{Sufficiency Failure}
\index{failure modes!sufficiency}

Sufficiency failure occurs when $d_\pi < d_Q$: the compression retains less branch structure than the query requires. The reconstruction operator $F_{d_Q}$ cannot be well-defined because the compression collapses distinct trajectories that the query distinguishes.

\textbf{Remedy: Increase $d_\pi$ until $d_\pi \geq d_Q$.} \textbf{Classification: Architectural.}

\section{Approximation Failure}
\index{failure modes!approximation}

Approximation failure occurs when the query has significant interaction contributions at depths far exceeding any tractable truncation order. Even with perfect stability and sufficiency, $E_a$ remains large.

\textbf{Remedy: Increase $d_Q$ or switch to a better-adapted approximation family.} \textbf{Classification: Architectural.}

\section{Misalignment Failure}
\index{failure modes!misalignment}

Misalignment failure occurs when the compression preserves structure calibrated to a different mechanism than the stability sequence respects. Three forms: depth misalignment ($d_\pi < d_Q$), over-compression ($d_\pi > d_Q$), and structural misalignment (equivalence relation misoriented relative to stable directions).

\textbf{Remedy: Redesign the compression around the stability mechanism.} \textbf{Classification: Architectural.}

\section{Degeneracy Failure}
\index{failure modes!degeneracy}\index{degeneracy!Type I}\index{degeneracy!Type II}

Degeneracy failure is introduced by measure-theoretic compressions and has no analogue in discrete settings.

\textbf{Type I (collapsing).} The compression identifies trajectories the query family treats as distinct. Information is destroyed. Examples: false memories, historical revisionism.

\textbf{Type II (conditioning).} The compression formally achieves $\varepsilon_1 = 0$ but the reconstruction operator is ill-conditioned: small perturbations in query-distinguishable trajectories produce large reconstruction output changes. Examples: accessibility skeleton degeneracy, dark-sector parameter degeneracy in cosmology.

\textbf{Remedy: Enrich the equivalence relation or measure structure.} \textbf{Classification: Structural-Architectural boundary.}
